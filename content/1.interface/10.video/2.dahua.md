A club camera does two separate jobs. It **sends live video** to SportyPlus as a
match is played, and — on Dahua cameras registered with **DoLynk Cloud** — it
**keeps its own copy** on an SD card inside the camera.

The second job is what saves you when the club's internet drops mid-match. Video
sent live and lost is gone; video on the card can be fetched afterwards, and the
match arrives complete, just a little later.

This page covers both, in the order you would do them.

::note
Only Dahua models supported by DoLynk Cloud can keep the second copy. Cameras
from other manufacturers still stream to SportyPlus — they just have nothing to
fall back on, so a dropped connection loses that footage permanently.
::

## What you need

- a supported Dahua camera, already initialised (using Dahua's **ConfigTool** or
  their mobile app) and reachable on the venue network
- an **SD card fitted**, sized for the retention you want — at the quality below a
  128 GB card holds roughly a week of continuous recording
- the camera added to your DoLynk Cloud account
- the camera's web interface password
- the camera's **stream address**, which SportyPlus gives you once the camera
  exists in Club Admin

## The short version

If you know your way around a Dahua camera, this is the whole configuration:

| Where | Setting | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Camera → Encode | Compression | **H.264** — never H.265 |
| Camera → Encode | Smart Codec | **Off** |
| Camera → Encode | Resolution | **1920×1080** |
| Camera → Encode | Frame Rate | **30** (25 in PAL installs), fixed |
| Camera → Encode | Bit Rate Type | **CBR** |
| Camera → Encode | Bit Rate | **4096 Kb/s** (never above 6144) |
| Camera → Encode | I Frame Interval | **30** — equal to the frame rate |
| Camera → Encode | SVC | **1 (off)** |
| Camera → Audio | Compression | **AAC** — no other codec works |
| Network → RTMP | Stream Type | **Main Stream** |
| Network → RTMP | Address Type | **Custom** |
| Network → RTMP | Custom Address | the stream address from Club Admin |
| Record → Record control | Record Mode | **Auto** |
| Record → Record control | Max Duration | **30–60 minutes** |
| Record → Storage | Storage Method | **Local storage** |
| Record → Storage | Disk Full | **Overwrite** |
| System → Date & Time | Time Zone | **UTC**, DST off |

Everything below explains why, and what goes wrong when one of them is missed.

## Video quality

These live in **Camera → Encode**, on the **Main Stream** side. The main stream is
the one that gets sent and recorded; the sub stream is only used for previews.

### I Frame Interval — the one nobody expects to matter

Set it **equal to the frame rate**: 30 at 30fps, 25 at 25fps. That is one
keyframe per second.

A camera only lets a viewer join at a *keyframe*, and live video is cut into
chunks on keyframe boundaries. A camera sending a keyframe once every 25 seconds
produces 25-second chunks, and live view ends up **more than a minute behind** the
court — watchable, but useless to anyone standing beside it.

One second between keyframes brings that down to a few seconds, and costs a little
bitrate and nothing else. Do not go above it: a 2-second interval means roughly
2-second chunks and about 6 seconds of delay, and it grows from there.

::warning
A camera can be set to a short interval and still not honour it. **Smart Codec**
(also called H.264+) overrides it, and so can a stream profile that was set up
somewhere other than this page. After changing it, check that live view actually
caught up rather than assuming the setting took.
::

::note
30 is also the lowest this field accepts on these cameras. A smaller number can
be written through the API without complaint and leaves the camera publishing a
stream nothing can play — so set this in the camera, or through SportyPlus, and
then check live view still works.
::

### Compression — H.264, never H.265

::warning
**H.265 breaks live video completely, and silently.** The camera connects, the
stream is accepted, everything looks healthy from the club's side — and no
picture ever arrives. There is no error anywhere to point at it.

If a camera streams nothing at all and every other setting looks right, this is
the first thing to check.
::

Set **Compression** to **H.264**. Encode Mode **H.264H (High)** is the default and
is right; **H.264B (Baseline)** is also fine and slightly faster to display.

### Smart Codec — off

Smart Codec saves storage by holding back keyframes when nothing in the picture
moves. On a court between points, that is most of the time.

It is the wrong trade here: it costs latency in live view, and it makes chunks
long and irregular, which is exactly what gap recovery struggles with. **Off.**

### Resolution, frame rate and bitrate

**1920×1080 at 30fps, 4096 Kb/s, CBR** is the baseline. It is a deliberate
starting point rather than a maximum — it looks good on a phone, it is kind to a
club uplink, and it is the profile the rest of the system is tuned around.

Higher is not automatically better. Nothing is re-encoded on the way to a viewer,
so every person watching pulls the full bitrate the camera sends: doubling it
doubles what the club's connection has to carry for each of them. **Do not go
above 6144 Kb/s** at this resolution without asking us first.

Avoid **4K**, and avoid **50 or 60fps**. Neither breaks anything by itself, but
they multiply what every viewer downloads, and the vendor profiles that ship with
them tend to carry long keyframe intervals — which is how a camera ends up more
than a minute behind without anybody changing a setting.

Use a **fixed** frame rate. Variable frame rate makes chunk lengths uneven, and
uneven chunks slow live view down for everyone watching that court.

::note
**CBR**, not VBR. A constant bitrate is predictable for the club's connection and
for us. VBR spikes on movement, which is precisely when a match is worth watching,
and nothing smooths those spikes out before they reach a viewer.
::

## Audio

If the camera has a microphone, set **Camera → Audio → Compression** to **AAC**.

::warning
**Only AAC works.** Dahua cameras often default to **G.711A** or **G.726**, and
those are accepted by everything along the way — the camera connects, the video
plays, and the sound is simply never there. No error appears anywhere, so it
reads as a broken microphone rather than a setting.

If a club reports "the camera has no sound", check this before touching the
hardware.
::

A camera with no microphone at all is fine. It streams and records normally,
without an audio track.

## Live streaming

The camera pushes its video to SportyPlus itself. Get the address from
**Club Admin → Commercial → Cameras**, open the camera, and copy the **stream
address** shown there. It looks like:

```
rtmp://stream1.sporty.plus/stream/<playback-id>?key=<stream-key>
```

Then in the camera, under **Setting → Network → RTMP**:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Enable | checked |
| Stream Type | **Main Stream** |
| Address Type | **Custom** |
| Custom Address | the address from Club Admin, copied exactly |

Leave the reconnect settings alone. A camera that drops and comes back is handled
for you, and an interrupted recording is stitched together afterwards.

::warning
**Stream Type must be Main Stream.** The quality settings above are set on the
main stream, so a camera pushing Sub Stream 1 sends none of them — and it looks
exactly like the settings did not take.
::

::warning
**The `?key=` part is not optional.** Without it, or with a single character
wrong, the stream is refused and the camera retries silently forever. The camera
shows no useful error, so a mistyped address looks like a network problem.

Every camera has its own address and its own key. They cannot be shared or reused
between cameras — two cameras on one key cannot both stream.
::

::warning
**The RTMP page is only reachable on the venue network.** The remote link
described below opens most of the camera's settings, but not its network
configuration — so the stream address has to be entered on site, or with Dahua's
ConfigTool, when the camera is first installed.
::

::note
The stream address contains a key that is specific to that camera. Treat it as a
password: anyone holding it can publish video to that court.
::

## Local recording — the second copy

::warning
**Four settings live only in the camera's own interface.** Get these wrong and
everything looks correct from SportyPlus — the card reports healthy, recording
appears enabled, the schedule looks right — while the camera writes nothing to
the card at all.

If footage is ever missing, check these first.
::

| Setting | Where | Set to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Storage Method | Record → Storage | **Local storage** |
| Record Mode | Record → Record control | **Auto** |
| Max Duration | Record → Record control | **30–60 minutes** |
| Time Zone | System → Date & Time | **UTC**, DST off |

### Storage Method — the one that catches people out

Some cameras arrive set to **network storage (SFTP upload)**, which sends
recordings to a server elsewhere instead of the card. The card then stays empty
and there is nothing to recover from.

Set it to **Local storage**, and set **Disk Full** to **Overwrite** so a full card
keeps recording over its oldest footage instead of stopping.

### Record Mode

**Auto** makes the camera follow the recording schedule SportyPlus sends it.

- **Manual** records continuously and ignores the schedule
- **Off** records nothing, whatever the schedule says

### Max Duration

The camera writes video in chunks, and a chunk can only be retrieved once it is
finished. This setting decides how quickly a gap can be repaired.

At 120 minutes, footage from a 6pm match might not be recoverable until 8pm.
SportyPlus keeps trying, so the recording still completes — it just takes longer.
Shorter chunks recover faster but create more files. **30–60 minutes** is a
sensible balance.

### Time Zone

Set the camera to **UTC with daylight saving off**.

Recordings are requested from the camera by time, so a camera whose clock is an
hour out returns the wrong hour. SportyPlus checks the camera's clock against its
own recordings and corrects for a mismatch, but putting every camera on UTC avoids
the problem instead of correcting it.

::note
Changing a camera's clock does not change recordings it has already made, so for
a while afterwards the card holds footage in both the old and new time. This is
handled automatically and settles on its own.
::

## Connecting it to SportyPlus

In **Club Admin → Commercial → Cameras**, create or edit the camera and set:

- **Camera type** — *DoLynk (Dahua) device*
- **Device serial number** — from the camera or DoLynk Cloud
- **Channel** — `0` for a single-lens camera
- **Court** — the court the camera actually points at

::warning
Double-check the court. A camera mapped to the wrong court records the wrong
match, and nobody notices until someone watches one back — by which point the
footage they wanted is gone.
::

Save, then reopen the camera. A **Camera device** panel appears, reading live from
the hardware.

## In the Camera device panel

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Recording status | Whether the card has anything recent on it — see below |
| SD card | How much of the card is in use |
| Record to SD card | Master switch. With this off, the schedule below does nothing |
| Local recording schedule | When the camera records to its own card |
| Flip image | For a camera mounted upside down |
| Illuminator brightness | Per lighting scene, where the camera supports it |
| On-screen text | Text burnt into the picture, so it appears in recordings too |
| Video quality | Resolution, frame rate, bitrate and keyframe interval per stream |
| Start / Stop streaming | Turns the live push on or off |
| Sync recordings | Re-checks the camera for recordings SportyPlus has not collected |
| Format SD card | Erases everything on the card |
| Open the camera's own settings | Opens the camera's interface from anywhere |

Set the schedule to cover your **opening hours**. Outside those hours the camera
records nothing locally, so nothing can be recovered from that period.

Both the switch and the schedule must be right. If a schedule is set while the
switch is off, the panel says so.

### Recording status

The line above the settings answers a different question from the rest of the
panel: not *what is this camera configured to do*, but *is it doing it*.

- **Recording to the card** — with the time of the newest footage found
- **This camera is not recording to its card** — the card has nothing recent on
  it, whatever the settings say

The second one means gap recovery cannot help this court right now. It usually
points at Storage Method or Record Mode above.

### Open the camera's own settings

This opens the camera's own interface through DoLynk, from anywhere — no need to
be at the club. It is how you reach the settings SportyPlus cannot change:
Smart Codec, storage method, record mode, chunk length and the clock.

It does **not** reach the camera's network configuration, so the RTMP stream
address still has to be set on the venue network.

## Checking it works

1. **Live view** — open the camera in Club Admin. The picture should appear within
   a few seconds and stay **a handful of seconds** behind real time. If it is a
   minute or more behind, the I Frame Interval is too long, or Smart Codec is on.
2. **The card is filling** — leave the camera recording for a few minutes and
   reopen the panel. Recording status should say *Recording to the card*, and the
   SD card figure should be climbing.
3. **A real recording** — play back a reservation recorded on that court.

::note
Formatting the card does **not** fix a camera that is not recording. If nothing is
being written, the cause is in the settings above — formatting only erases footage
you might have wanted.
::

## When a recording has a gap

If the connection drops mid-match, the recording shows one of three things:

- **Recovering missing footage** — the missing part is being fetched from the
  camera and will appear shortly
- **This recording has a gap** — part of the match was not captured
- **Some footage is missing for good** — the connection dropped and the camera had
  no copy either, usually because it was not recording locally at the time

The first is normal and resolves on its own. The third means the settings on this
page were not in place when the match was played.

## If something is wrong

| What you see | Usually means |
| --- | --- |
| Live view is a minute or more behind | I Frame Interval too long, or Smart Codec on |
| Live view delay got worse on its own | The camera is dropping keyframes — see below |
| Live view never appears, stream looks connected | Compression is H.265, not H.264 |
| Live view never appears at all | RTMP address wrong, or the camera cannot reach the internet |
| Video plays but there is no sound | Audio compression is not AAC |
| Quality settings appear to do nothing | RTMP Stream Type is Sub Stream, not Main Stream |
| Live view stutters or drops out | Bitrate too high for the club's upload |
| *Not recording to its card*, card healthy | Storage Method is not Local storage, or Record Mode is Off |
| Recordings have gaps that are never repaired | Camera was not recording locally when the match was played |
| Nothing can be recovered from this camera | No SD card fitted, or the card has failed |

::note
**Why delay can drift upward on its own.** Live view runs as far behind as the
*longest* chunk in the last few seconds, not the average. A camera that mostly
sends keyframes on time but occasionally misses one will sit at the slower figure
until it stops missing them.

So live view that "randomly got worse" is usually the camera's keyframes, not the
club's internet.
::
